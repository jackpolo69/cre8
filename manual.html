0.5 · MIDI — plug in and play

Hit MIDI in the transport and every connected controller comes alive (Chrome/Edge). MPC note map: notes 36–51 (C1–D#2) fire pads 1–16; 52 and up play KEYS melodically around middle C; below 36 plays BASS. Velocity is honored everywhere.

  • MIDI • n shows how many devices are live. Click again to arm MIDI REC (red): while the transport runs, everything you play quantizes straight into the grid and pad patterns — finger-drum a beat in, play a bassline in, it lands as steps.
  • SPACEBAR is play/stop, anywhere — the FL reflex.

1 · PRODUCE — the synesthetic grid

The grid is the instrument. Six voices, sixteen steps, and the color language is the map — kick + snare in orange, hats in yellow, long tones (bass, keys) in purple and teal. Brighter means harder; dimmer means softer. Swing grays the off-beats. Read the colors, and you're reading the record.

GestureResult
Click a stepToggle it on or off (full velocity).
⇧ + clickCycle velocity: 100% → 60% → 30%. The step dims as it softens.
Right-clickOn BASS and KEYS: cycle the pitch through the scale (+2, +3, +5, +7, −2, −5 semitones). The offset shows on the step.

STARTER / CLEARReload the built-in groove, or wipe the current bank.
COPY → NEXTDuplicate this bank into the next one — build A, copy to B, mutate B. That's arrangement.

The Stereo Scope

Below the grid, the scope draws the finished master — left channel over right, live. When the low end is mono and tight, the two traces move together. When the mix opens up, they breathe apart.

The Frequency Reader

The reader shows the live spectrum, the centroid in Hz (where the energy of your record is vibrating right now), its 7-band name — SUB, LOW, LOW-MID, MID, HIGH-MID, HIGH, AIR — and the master compressor's gain reduction. Produce with your eyes on the vibration.

The Machavelli method: lay the drums first in orange and yellow, feel the swing at 54–58%, then paint the purple. When the centroid sits where the record's soul should live, print it.

1.5 · THE SONG — arrangement mode

Patterns become records here. THE SONG is a chain of bars — each chip is one bar playing one bank (A–D).

  • Click a chip to cycle which bank that bar plays. Right-click removes it. + add bar appends the current bank.
  • SONG MODE on: press play and the transport walks the arrangement bar by bar — the bank buttons follow along so you can watch the song move.
  • ⭳ BOUNCE SONG prints the ENTIRE arrangement — every bar, pads included — through the full master chain to one mastered WAV. This is your record.
Structure like a producer: A A B A is verse–verse–chorus–verse. Build bank A as the verse groove, B as the chorus lift, C as the bridge, D as the breakdown.

1.7 · ARRANGE — the session timeline

The ARRANGE screen is the Logic-style session view: tracks down the left, 32 bars across, a live playhead walking the timeline as it plays.

  • ADD TRACK — one per instrument (KICK … KEYS): each cell plays that instrument's row from a bank; click a cell to cycle A → B → C → D → off. + PADS runs the pad sequence on its bars. + AUDIO makes an audio track — pick a clip source (any take, import, or stem) and click a bar to place it.
  • ARRANGE MODE on — the transport plays the timeline instead of the loop; the playhead moves in real time and every cell stays editable while it runs.
The workflow: build banks in PRODUCE, split a record in STEMS, drop its vocal on an AUDIO track, arrange verses and choruses per instrument — the session takes shape bar by bar.

1.8 · NOTES — stems become MIDI

In STEMS, hit → MIDI on any stem and the transcriber reads it — onsets plus pitch tracking — into notes on the score grid (piano roll). ▶ PLAY hears them, → STEP GRID quantizes the busiest bar straight into the step grid (drums to their lanes, melody to BASS/KEYS with semitone offsets), and ⭳ .MID exports a real MIDI file any DAW opens. Kick/snare/hat stems land on GM notes 36/38/42.

Every kit sound and every upload lives in THE BOX, persisted on your machine. Each sound can be assigned to an armed pad, downloaded (⭳) as a file, or deleted (✕). Your uploads glow gold.

Legal, in writing: the built-in kits are original synthesized sounds — they're yours to use in anything. Your uploads stay on your machine; nothing is sent anywhere.

CHOKE GROUPS: shift-click a pad to cycle its choke group (C1–C3, badge on the pad). Pads in the same group cut each other off — the classic open-hat / closed-hat move: closed hat chokes the open hat mid-ring, exactly like an MPC.

2.7 · STEMS — take any record apart

STEMS lives in the TRACK screen — it is part of the creation process. Load a stereo master and GENIUS ∞ breaks it into named stems: KICK · SNARE + CLAPS · HI HATS · BASS · LEAD VOCAL · MUSIC — band-split transient separation plus center-channel extraction, computed in the box; nothing leaves your machine.

  • → TRACK drops a stem into TRACKING as an audio track (so does ALL → TRACKS for the whole set). → PAD makes it a playable sample. → DECK A/B throws it on a turntable. ⭳ WAV exports the file, named.
  • Treat a long stem like a sample — that is the move. Your catalog in stereo becomes raw material again: pull the vocal, keep the music bed, rebuild the record.
Honest about quality: this engine is classic DSP separation — strong on kick/snare/hats/bass/vocal-center, approximate on dense mixes. Instrument-level naming (piano vs backing vocals vs ad libs) needs neural separation, which is the headline feature of the desktop edition in progress. Nothing here pretends otherwise.

4 · MIX — the console

Six hardware strips, one per voice. Top to bottom, exactly like a desk:

SectionWhat it does
MeterLive level in the track's color.
FaderChannel level, up to +40% over unity.
PANTrue stereo placement.
LOW / MID / HIGH3-band EQ, ±12dB — shelves on the ends, a bell at 1.2k.
VERBSend to the shared reverb — a real convolution room generated inside the box.
DYN · THR · RATPer-strip compressor. DYN switches it in, THR sets where it bites (−40 to 0dB), RAT sets how hard (1:1 to 10:1). The red LED lights when it's working.
M / SMute and Solo. Solo follows console rules — any solo silences everything unsoloed.
Fast mix recipe: DYN the kick (THR −20, RAT 4) and the bass (THR −24, RAT 3), pull hats to 80%, pan keys slightly off-center, one breath of VERB on snare. Watch the scope tighten.

THE FX SENDS: AUX 1 is the reverb send; AUX 2 is the tempo ECHO send. The master section's FX RETURNS block picks the reverb room — ROOM / HALL / PLATE / CATHEDRAL — and holds the echo's TIME, feedback (FB), and both return levels. Verb for space, echo for depth; a touch of AUX 2 on the snare is the oldest trick in the book.

4.5 · THE RACK — plugin inserts

Under the console lives THE RACK: per-channel insert chains, FL-channel-rack style. Pick a channel, stack devices left to right — each one is a plugin window with its own knobs, sitting after the strip's EQ and DYN, and every device prints in the bounce.

  • CHORUS (rate/depth/mix) · PHASER (rate/mix) · DRIVE (drive/level) · CRUSH (bits/mix) · AUTOFILTER (rate/range) · TREMOLO (rate/depth)
  • Racks save with the project and load back exactly.
On VST: a browser can't execute native VST3 binaries — no honest web app can. THE RACK is the socket architecture; the desktop edition of GENIUS ∞ docks real VST3s into these same slots.

5 · MASTER — the rack

Three units of gear, top to bottom, in the exact order the signal flows:

UnitControls
MASTER EQLOW / MID / HIGH, ±10dB — the tone of the whole record.
COMPRESSORTHRESH, RATIO, ATTACK, RELEASE — glue. The GR meter shows exactly how many dB it's holding, live.
LIMIT & OUTThe limiter (−1.5dB brickwall, on/off) and final OUTPUT level.

⭳ BOUNCE 4-BAR WAV

The money button. It renders four bars of the current bank offline, through this exact chain — every strip setting, the reverb, the EQ, the compressor, the limiter — and hands you a true 44.1k stereo WAV file. What you hear is what you print. That file is mastered by this rack.

TAPE DRIVE is analog glue — soft saturation before the compressor; a little (10–25%) warms and thickens, a lot distorts on purpose. ST WIDTH is a mid/side control: MONO checks club translation, NATURAL is untouched, wider spreads the sides. LOUD is the live loudness meter of the finished master — aim for it breathing in the middle of the bar, not pinned.

A ↔ BEqual-power crossfader. Both decks flow through the master chain, so the reader and scope see your set too.
Blend move: match PITCH by ear, kill the incoming LOW, ride the fader to center, then swap the lows. The platters show you both tempos at a glance.

SCRATCH: grab a platter and drag left/right — the record scrubs under a needle with real scratch grains. Release and it rolls back into play from where you left it.

DJ FX (per deck): the FX row under each deck is the performance unit — one-knob FILTER (left = low-pass sweep, right = high-pass sweep, center = open), beat ECHO with time + wet, VERB wet, and FLANGE wet. Kill the filter into an echo-out and drop the other deck — that's the transition.

THE PRO DECK: every loaded track gets its BPM detected automatically (shown live, rate-adjusted). SYNC matches a deck's tempo to the other deck — beatmatch in one press. The pitch fader range switches 8 / 16 / 50%. LOOP 1/2/4/8 sets an instant beat loop from where you are (EXIT releases it clean); JUMP ±1/±4 beat-skips through the track. The VINYL/JOG toggle is the CDJ platter switch: VINYL means touching the platter stops the record and drags scratch it; JOG means dragging bends pitch to nudge a blend into phase. CHOP in the FX row is the beat-slicer — synced to the deck's BPM at 1/4, 1/8 or 1/16 with a depth knob.

THE MIXER (middle): a real two-channel battle mixer — per channel: TRIM, full 3-band EQ (−24 to +12 dB) with KILL switches, CUE to the headphone bus, VU meter, and a vertical channel fader; the crossfader underneath multiplies against the channel faders exactly like the hardware. Drop any audio file straight onto a platter to load it. The ■ button is the Technics stop — the platter brakes to rest instead of cutting dead.

Hardware: MIDI controllers connect today through the transport's MIDI button. Full DJ-controller mapping (jog wheels, mixers, HID decks) docks in with the desktop edition.
